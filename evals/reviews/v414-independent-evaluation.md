# 4.1.4 Cross-Section Writing Evaluation

## Procedure And Scope

One fresh responding context received the frozen public 4.1.3 instructions and three fictional author requests. It read no scoring rubric, prior evaluation, private manuscript or experimental data. The requests asked for related manuscript sections, not isolated synonym substitutions. The same respondent then received revised 4.1.4 instructions and five requests: the original three plus two positive-control cases for genuine parameter updating and an explicitly defined quality-relevance decision.

The runtime did not expose a model identifier. Semantic judgments are the maintainer's review of actual independent responses, not another independent grader or a statistical benchmark. Same-context revised-source responses are not new fresh-context runs. No real paper, experiment, deployment or acceptance outcome is tested.

## Baseline Observations

All three baseline responses preserved the supplied technical meaning and produced usable linked prose. The grouping case retained variable-level updates, unit pooling, topology-biased scores, variable-conditioned gates and the unchanged encoder path. The quality case distinguished process-fault classes from product inspection categories and did not force a second contribution. The irregular-sampling case retained interval information, the training-only reconstruction role and the accepted regression-head sentence.

The baseline abstract's stated 186 whitespace-delimited words were independently counted. Its detailed mechanism account fits the requested scope; it does not establish a universally preferred abstract length or sentence pattern.

The source changes clarify distinctions that were distributed across existing guides and add locatable published examples. The baseline did not show a scientific failure to repair. Any claim of a measured before/after improvement would therefore exceed this evidence.
