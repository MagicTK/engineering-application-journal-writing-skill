# An imposed imbalance on a fictional rotor rig

An operator moves a test mass to a prescribed eccentric position at time zero and holds it there. The mass fixture and its position log exist only on the rig. The intended monitoring system receives vibration velocity and shaft speed. Both balanced and unbalanced runs are collected at each speed. At seconds 0 through 5, the six successive vibration velocity RMS readings are 2.0, 3.1, 4.0, 4.1, 4.1, and 4.1 mm/s. For this exercise the experimental plan defines a settled vibration response as three consecutive one-second changes of at most 0.2 mm/s in absolute value.

## What the records support

- **Diagnosis inputs.** Use the signals available to the intended monitoring system. The fixture log identifies the imposed imbalance and its timing for evaluation. Shaft speed is available in service in both classes and can be included if the model uses it.
- **Settling time.** The first two changes are 1.1 and 0.9 mm/s. The next three are 0.1, 0.0, and 0.0 mm/s. The vibration signal first meets the specified criterion at second 5. Describe the scope as the measured vibration response. The six readings alone give no settling time for another process variable.
- **Evaluation interval.** For detection delay measured from the imposed change, retain the eligible transient readings and count elapsed time from second 0. For a steady-response comparison using this criterion, the first eligible endpoint is second 5. If the unit assessed is a complete window of settled readings, its start and end must also satisfy the window rule stated for that comparison.

## Applying the distinction to a published apparatus

The Case Western Reserve University bearing study describes single-point defects introduced by electrical discharge machining and vibration recordings collected from accelerometers on the rig. Those descriptions identify how the defect was prepared and how the bearing response was observed. A comparison among bearing conditions can use the prepared defect type as a label. The apparatus description supplies no in-record instant at which the machined defect first arose. A claim about elapsed time since physical fault onset would require separate timing evidence.
