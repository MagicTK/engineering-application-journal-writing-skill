## Held-out simulated scenario — Water-quality soft sensing with maintenance costs

**This scenario is constructed for this review. It contains no observed plant facts, measured results, literature novelty finding, or empirical validation.** Assume a proposed paper estimates effluent ammonium concentration from online water-quality measurements. Sensors can drift between cleaning/calibration events; laboratory assays are delayed and incur cost. The proposed contribution is a calibration-age-aware estimator update with a budgeted assay policy. Assume historical sensor, assay-availability and maintenance records are potentially available but have not been inspected.

1. **Route and contribution.** Record the object as a wastewater treatment unit, task as concentration estimation for operator decisions, challenge as drift and delayed paid labels, candidate method as calibration-aware updating, and available evidence as unverified historical records. Start with the background-driven archetype. Treat this as a familiar soft-sensing task with a proposed engineering adaptation or mechanism; do not call it a new problem just because maintenance cost and drift coexist. A new coupled-formulation claim would additionally need evidence that their interaction changes the feasible information/decision problem relative to nearest prior formulations.
2. **Fair positioning and claim.** Recover sources for established soft sensors, drift adaptation and budgeted measurement policies. Record achievements and actual input/cost assumptions before selecting the nearest alternatives. Proposed question: does calibration-aware updating improve concentration error at the same assay budget, or reduce modeled assay cost at a fixed declared error target? Neither outcome is yet known. Include the unadapted estimator and relevant competitive updating/assay policies under matched information access; do not require every published model to be beaten.
3. **Minimum evidence and student handoff.** Supply data/code/config versions, a chronological campaign-level split manifest, assay measurement and availability timestamps, train-only preprocessing, seed and model-selection rules, allowed online updating, authorized budget, and stop conditions. Whole laboratory samples and repeated measurements from one event remain grouped. The student returns predictions/targets, event IDs, model-update/assay decisions, logs, configuration, failure/deviation notes and cost inputs. Until code/data are inspected, implementation steps and any commands remain untested. Completion requires the files and checks, not improved performance.
4. **Verification and coupled-constraint test.** Recompute MAE in declared physical units, inspect error across maintenance age, and document actual assay counts and the provenance of unit costs. Compare fixed and varying drift conditions under fixed and varying measurement budgets if an interaction is claimed. A retrospective policy replay has an information-access problem if it queries labels absent from the log; restrict the comparison to supported observations or identify the need for an appropriate prospective/simulated study. Modeled costs are not realized plant savings. An author's statement that runs finished remains reported-unverified until artifacts support the relevant checks; recover existing exports before requesting reruns.
5. **Prose and figures.** A hypothesis-level manuscript sentence could be: “This study examines whether calibration-age-aware updates improve effluent ammonium estimation under a fixed laboratory-assay budget.” This states the planned question without asserting success or novelty. The conceptual sensing→assay availability→update→estimate diagram follows AstraDraw render/iteration, scoped author acceptance or explicit delegated choice, native PPT reconstruction, edit trials, human revision as master, approved export and local TeX inspection. Error-versus-maintenance-age and cost/error plots wait for verified predictions and cost data; no generated curve supplies evidence.
6. **Reviewer response and stop.** For a request to demonstrate maintenance savings, distinguish retrospective estimated cost from actual operational savings. Use a verified cost analysis if available, narrow the claim where appropriate, or keep a specifically owned prospective test pending. State completed manuscript edits only with verified locations. Handoff can be complete for an authorized planning task while empirical claims and the figure-to-TeX loop remain unexecuted.
