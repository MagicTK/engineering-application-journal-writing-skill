**3. 英文段落**
A · Methods
The fixed diagnostic classifier retained all 1,000 scalar weights during conversion from FP32 storage to signed 8-bit weight codes, accompanied by sixteen FP32 scales and sixteen signed 32-bit zero points. Parameter payload was defined as these weight and metadata arrays alone. Input measurements were separately standardized using training-set means and standard deviations and retained in FP32. Classification accuracy was evaluated on the same held-out decisions by dequantizing the weight codes before ordinary floating-point matrix multiplication.

A · Results
Conversion reduced the exported parameter payload from 4,000 to 1,128 bytes, saving 2,872 bytes (71.8%) while preserving the 1,000-weight architecture. The converted payload occupied 28.2% of the original size, corresponding to an original-to-converted size ratio of approximately 3.546:1. Held-out classification accuracy changed from 96.0% to 95.8%. Thus, the observed storage–accuracy trade-off was a 71.8% reduction in parameter payload with a 0.2-percentage-point decrease in accuracy under the floating-point evaluation path.

B · Methods
During training, the diagnostic classifier simulated an eight-level weight representation using \(q=\operatorname{clip}(\operatorname{round}(w/s),-4,3)\) and \(\hat w=sq\), with \(s=0.25\). The reconstructed weights were used in the forward pass, and backpropagation employed a declared surrogate derivative through the discrete mapping. All fitting arithmetic and stored master weights remained FP32, while the training examples and their measured class labels were unchanged.
