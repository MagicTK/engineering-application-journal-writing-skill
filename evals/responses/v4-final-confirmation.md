I would treat this as **one local label correction and a separate table-layout diagnosis**. Neither requires AstraDraw or image-to-editable-ppt. Their absence does not justify installation, conversion, redrawing, or changing your approved style.

**What I need**
- The current approved vector master and its reference export, with the affected arrow identified. Vector artwork does not necessarily contain editable text, so I would check the actual object structure.
- The relevant method description, including any equation or notation defining what that arrow carries. Without it, I cannot determine the correct replacement label.
- The table source, relevant formatting definitions, and its current rendered page, plus the intended column/page width and permitted layout adjustments. No raw research data are needed for this formatting diagnosis.

**What I would do next**
1. **Confirm the correction against the method.** Identify the arrow’s source, destination and meaning, then present the existing label, proposed replacement and supporting method passage. A mislabeled arrow does not automatically authorize changing its direction or endpoints.
2. **Continue from the approved master.** In a later authorized edit, use a compatible available vector editor or source-level editing route to change only that label. Preserve Times New Roman, its face and size, the fixed palette, geometry, connector routing and all other objects. Verify the actual font rather than accepting a look-alike. If the label is outlined or the required font is unavailable, request the necessary native source or font access instead of silently substituting or rebuilding.
3. **Diagnose table width without shrinking text.** Inspect long headings, column specifications and available width. First propose defined heading abbreviations, header wrapping or revised column allocation within the permitted layout scope. Preserve metrics, values, precision, ordering, grouping and approved font sizes. Do not use whole-table scaling or smaller fonts to circumvent those locks.
4. **Escalate only the unresolved layout choice.** If those measures cannot fit the table, present options such as full-width placement or an approved split that retains all evidence. Splitting or moving material to supplementary content requires your decision; overflow is not permission to delete metrics.
5. **Verify after any future authorized edit.** Compare the local label and full figure against the approved baseline, verify vector/font preservation, and inspect the rendered table at final reading size. Check compilation warnings and the document layout, especially affected and adjacent pages. Successful compilation alone would not establish visual correctness.
